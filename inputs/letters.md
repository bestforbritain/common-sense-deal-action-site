# Common Sense Deal action site: draft letters

Draft · 26 November 2026 · Joshua Edwicker

## How these letters work

Every letter asks the recipient to support EU membership, and only the sector paragraph changes between sectors. The MP and Minister versions share the same sector paragraph and differ only in the opening and the ask.

The site assembles each letter in this order:

1. Opening (MP or Minister version)
2. Sector paragraph
3. Personal paragraph, if the sender writes one
4. The ask (MP or Minister version)
5. Sign-off

The site fills in these tokens:

| Token | What it becomes |
| --- | --- |
| {mpAddressAs} | The MP's name as Parliament says to address them, e.g. "Ms Smith" |
| {constituency} | The constituency found from the business postcode |
| {sectorNoun} | The short sector description listed with each sector below |
| {senderName}, {role}, {businessName} | From the sender's form |
| {businessAddress} | From the sender's form, prefilled with their postcode |

Drafting rules:

- Sector paragraphs describe the sector, not the sender's own business, so nobody signs a claim that isn't true for them. Their own experience goes in the personal paragraph.
- The only figures are the two Frontier Economics figures in the ask.
- A posted letter must fit on one A4 sheet, so each sector paragraph stays under about 90 words.

## Shared parts

These parts are the same in all 13 letters. Both versions ask for EU membership; the Minister version asks the government to signal that the UK intends to rejoin.

### Opening: MP

Dear {mpAddressAs},

I am writing to you as {role} of {businessName}, a {sectorNoun} business in {constituency}, to ask you to support the UK rejoining the European Union.

### Opening: Minister

Dear Minister,

I am writing as {role} of {businessName}, a {sectorNoun} business in {constituency}, to urge the government to set the UK on a path back to EU membership.

### The ask: MP

Independent modelling by Frontier Economics for Best for Britain found that EU membership could boost the UK economy by at least £92bn and add £43bn a year in exports. For businesses like mine, it would mean fewer forms, lower costs and easier access to our biggest market.

Please will you support the UK rejoining the EU, and press the government to signal that we intend to rejoin? I would be grateful to know what you will do.

### The ask: Minister

Independent modelling by Frontier Economics for Best for Britain found that EU membership could boost the UK economy by at least £92bn and add £43bn a year in exports. That is far more than any trade deal the UK has signed since leaving the EU.

I urge the government to signal that the UK intends to rejoin the European Union. I would welcome a reply setting out what the government will do.

### Sign-off

Yours sincerely,

{senderName}

{role}, {businessName}

{businessAddress}

### Personal paragraph prompt

Label: How has the current deal with the EU affected your business? (optional)

Hint: For example, orders you've lost, paperwork you now fill in, or staff you can't recruit. Up to 1,000 characters.

### Email subject line (MP email only)

Default: A {constituency} business asking you to back EU membership

Alternative: {businessName} on EU membership

## Sector paragraphs

Each sector has one paragraph, used in both the MP and Minister letters, plus the short description that fills {sectorNoun} in the opening.

### Food and drink production

{sectorNoun}: food and drink

Since the UK left the EU, food and drink producers have faced export health certificates, vet sign-offs and border checks on goods that used to move freely. For fresh and chilled products, every hour of delay costs money. The food and drink agreement signed with the EU in November is a welcome step, but it will take time to come into force and does not remove customs declarations or rules-of-origin paperwork. Only EU membership would remove these barriers entirely.

### Farming, fishing and forestry

{sectorNoun}: farming, fishing or forestry

Farming, fishing and forestry businesses have been hit from two sides. Selling into the EU now means health certificates, checks and paperwork that hit perishable produce and seafood hardest, and some products were shut out of the EU market altogether. At the same time, the end of freedom of movement has made seasonal and skilled workers harder to recruit. The new food and drink agreement will ease some checks over time, but it does not cover recruitment. EU membership would deal with both.

### Manufacturing and engineering

{sectorNoun}: manufacturing

Manufacturers rely on parts and materials that cross the Channel, sometimes more than once before a product is finished. Since the UK left the EU, each crossing can mean customs declarations, rules-of-origin paperwork and delays at the border, and some firms now have to meet separate UK and EU product rules. These costs make UK manufacturers less competitive and push EU customers towards suppliers inside the single market. EU membership would remove the paperwork and put us back on equal terms.

### Automotive (making, selling or repairing vehicles)

{sectorNoun}: motor industry

The motor industry depends on fast, reliable supply chains between the UK and Europe. Since the UK left the EU, parts and finished vehicles face customs paperwork and rules-of-origin requirements, and delays at the border can hold up production lines, deliveries and repairs. Those costs are passed on to manufacturers, dealers, garages and, in the end, drivers. EU membership would let parts and vehicles move freely again.

### Wholesale, import and export

{sectorNoun}: wholesale and trading

Wholesalers, importers and exporters have felt the change more directly than almost anyone. Goods that once moved freely across the Channel now need customs declarations, broker fees and, for many products, border checks in both directions. Small consignments are hit hardest, because the paperwork costs the same whatever the size of the order, and many smaller firms have stopped selling to the EU altogether. EU membership would put us back inside the single market and customs union, with none of this paperwork.

### Retail

{sectorNoun}: retail

Retailers have faced higher costs and less choice since the UK left the EU. Stock from Europe now comes with customs paperwork, border checks on food and plants, and extra charges from suppliers and hauliers, which end up in the prices our customers pay. Some EU suppliers stopped shipping small orders to the UK, and selling online to customers in Europe has become harder. EU membership would bring down costs and give customers more choice.

### Transport, haulage and logistics

{sectorNoun}: transport and logistics

Transport and logistics firms deal with the cost of our current EU deal every day. Each load now needs customs and safety paperwork, and drivers face longer waits at ports and border controls. UK hauliers are limited in how many jobs they can do inside the EU, so more lorries come home empty. EU membership would end the paperwork, lift those limits and keep goods moving through ports like Dover.

### Hospitality and tourism

{sectorNoun}: hospitality

Hospitality and tourism businesses have struggled to recruit since freedom of movement ended, and many relied on staff from across Europe. Imported food and drink now costs more because of border checks and paperwork. European visitors also face more hurdles to come here, including a fee to apply for permission to travel. EU membership would bring back freedom of movement, cut the cost of supplies and make it easier for Europeans to visit.

### Arts, culture and events

{sectorNoun}: arts and events

Artists, performers and event companies used to tour Europe freely. Now each country can set its own rules on work permits, equipment needs extra paperwork to cross borders, and limits on how long UK citizens can stay in the EU cut tours short. Many smaller acts and crews have found that European work no longer pays. EU membership would restore free movement for people and equipment, and reopen European audiences to UK talent.

### Creative, media and digital

{sectorNoun}: creative and digital

Creative, media and digital businesses sell skills and ideas, and the EU is one of our biggest markets. Since the UK left, sending staff to work on projects in Europe means checking different rules in each country, and short stays are capped. Some firms have had to set up offices inside the EU just to keep serving clients there. EU membership would let our people and services move freely again.

### Professional and financial services

{sectorNoun}: professional services

Professional and financial services firms lost much of their easy access to EU clients when the UK left. Professional qualifications are no longer automatically recognised, financial firms lost the passporting rights that let them serve the whole EU from the UK, and staff travelling for work face different rules in each country. Many firms have moved staff and business into the EU to cope. EU membership would restore that access and help win back jobs and investment.

### Construction

{sectorNoun}: construction

Construction firms rely on skilled workers and on materials, many of which come from Europe. Since freedom of movement ended, recruiting tradespeople has become harder, and imported materials and equipment now come with customs paperwork, delays and extra costs that push up the price of every project. EU membership would help close the skills gap and cut the cost and delay of getting materials on site.

### Other

{sectorNoun}: local

Our current deal with the EU has added paperwork, delays and costs for businesses of every kind. Buying from and selling to Europe, our biggest trading partner, is harder than it was, and recruiting staff has become more difficult since freedom of movement ended. These costs hold back growth and end up in the prices people pay. EU membership would remove this unnecessary hassle and make it easier for businesses like mine to grow.

## Fact-check notes

Four claims have a source linked below; the other nine need one before sign-off, or should be softened or cut.

| Where | Claim | Source or what to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The ask (both) | Membership could boost the economy by at least £92bn and add £43bn a year in exports | [Frontier Economics for Best for Britain](https://bestforbritain.org/publication/eu-uk-integration/). Best for Britain's own release frames the £92bn as a GDP boost over 5–10 years ([release](https://www.bestforbritain.org/eu_membership_to_supercharge_uk_economy_by_at_least_92bn_report_suggests)). |
| The ask (Minister) | Far more than any trade deal the UK has signed since leaving | The release compares membership with the India deal (government estimate 0.13% of GDP). Check the government's estimates for CPTPP, Australia and New Zealand. |
| The ask (MP), Other | The EU is the UK's biggest trading partner | ONS trade figures. |
| Food and drink | The SPS agreement was signed in November, isn't yet in force, and doesn't remove customs declarations or rules of origin | [GOV.UK: UK/EU Summit 2026 key documents](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ukeu-summit-2026-key-documents), including the SPS explainer. |
| Farming | Some products were shut out of the EU market | Seed potatoes: the agreement reopens the EU market to them from 1 March 2028 ([Potato Pro](https://www.potatopro.com/news/2026/uk-eu-sps-agreement-reopens-eu-market-great-britain-seed-potatoes-starting-march-2028)). |
| Wholesale | Many smaller firms have stopped selling to the EU | [Centre for European Reform](https://www.cer.eu/node/12190): at least 20,000 small businesses have stopped trading with Europe. |
| Retail | Some EU suppliers stopped shipping small orders to the UK | Widely reported in 2021; find a source or cut. |
| Transport | UK hauliers are limited in EU jobs, so more lorries come home empty | Cabotage limits are in the Trade and Cooperation Agreement; "more lorries come home empty" needs a source (e.g. RHA) or cutting. |
| Hospitality | European visitors pay a fee to apply for permission to travel | GOV.UK guidance on the Electronic Travel Authorisation. |
| Arts | Many smaller acts and crews find European work no longer pays | Needs a source (e.g. ISM or Musicians' Union surveys) or cutting. |
| Creative | The EU is one of our biggest services markets; some firms set up EU offices | ONS services trade figures; broadcaster relocations. |
| Professional | Passporting lost, qualifications not automatically recognised, firms moved staff and business into the EU | Needs a source for the moves (e.g. EY's financial services Brexit tracker). |
| Construction | Many materials come from Europe | Needs a source (trade data on construction materials) or softening. |
